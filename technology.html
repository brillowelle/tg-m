Diagnostic Technology

Diagnostic Technology for Eye Health

Eye-care technology supports the examination of different structures and functions of the eye. Depending on the patient’s symptoms and clinical requirements, an examination may involve retinal imaging, eye pressure measurement, corneal assessment or specialised tests of eye movement and vision.

At Majan Eye Centre, our available equipment supports different aspects of ophthalmic assessment.

Our Equipment

Our Eye Care Equipment

Retinal Imaging and Examination

Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)

OCT produces detailed cross-sectional images of structures inside the eye, particularly the retina. It can help clinicians assess and monitor certain retinal conditions.

Fluorescein Angiography

This imaging technique uses a fluorescent dye and specialised photography to examine blood flow in retinal blood vessels when clinically indicated.

Indirect Ophthalmoscope

An instrument used to examine the retina and other structures at the back of the eye.

Eye Examination and Imaging

Modern Slit Lamps

Slit lamps provide magnified examination of the front structures of the eye, including the cornea, iris and lens.

Carl Zeiss Advanced Microscope

A microscope used in ophthalmic examination or procedures, depending on its specific configuration and clinical application.

Eye Ultrasound — B-Scan and A-Scan

B-scan ultrasound: Produces images of internal eye structures and may be useful when the retina or other structures cannot be clearly viewed directly.

A-scan ultrasound: Measures distances within the eye and can support certain ophthalmic measurements, including those used in cataract surgery planning.

Eye Pressure and Glaucoma Assessment

Computerized Non-Contact Tonometer

Measures eye pressure using a non-contact method.

Applanation Tonometer

Measures intraocular pressure using an applanation technique. Eye pressure measurement may form part of a glaucoma assessment.

Humphrey Perimeter

Tests the visual field to identify areas of reduced peripheral or central vision. It may be used when assessing and monitoring conditions such as glaucoma.

Corneal Assessment and Measurement

Automatic Kerato-Refractometer

Measures refractive error and estimates the curvature of the cornea to support vision assessment.

Pachymeter

Measures corneal thickness, which can be relevant to certain eye assessments, including glaucoma evaluation and refractive surgery screening.

Topography

Maps the shape and curvature of the cornea. Corneal topography can support the evaluation of selected corneal conditions and refractive surgery suitability.

Lensometer

Measures the optical power of spectacle lenses, helping assess existing glasses prescriptions.

Retinoscope

Helps clinicians estimate refractive error during an eye examination.

Eye Alignment and Vision Assessment

Synoptophore

An instrument used to assess eye alignment, binocular vision and certain aspects of how the eyes work together. It may support the assessment of squint and related conditions.

Laser Technology

Green Laser

Green laser systems can be used for selected retinal treatments when clinically indicated.

YAG Laser

A YAG laser is used for specific ophthalmic procedures, depending on the diagnosis and clinical indication.

Cataract and Refractive Surgery Technology

Alcon Centurion Phaco Machine

A phacoemulsification system used in cataract surgery. It supports the removal of a cataract through a specialised surgical technique.

LASIK Technology

LASIK is a type of refractive surgery used to correct certain vision problems. Patients should undergo a suitability assessment before considering the procedure.

How It Helps

How Our Technology Supports Eye Care

The equipment available at Majan Eye Centre supports different aspects of eye assessment and treatment planning.

The equipment used depends on the patient’s symptoms, examination findings and the clinician’s recommendations. Not every patient requires every test.

Our equipment supports:

  • Examination of the retina and internal eye structures.
  • Measurement of eye pressure and assessment of visual fields.
  • Evaluation of corneal shape and thickness.
  • Assessment of refractive error and spectacle lenses.
  • Examination of eye alignment and binocular vision.
  • Support for selected cataract and laser procedures.

Patient-Focused

Patient-Focused Eye Examinations

Technology is an important part of eye care, but diagnostic results must be interpreted alongside a patient’s symptoms, medical history and clinical examination.

The eye-care professional determines which tests are appropriate and explains the findings and recommended next steps.
