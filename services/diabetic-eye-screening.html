Why Diabetic Eye Screening?

Diabetes can damage the small blood vessels in the retina. This is called diabetic retinopathy and it is one of the leading causes of preventable sight loss in working-age adults.

In the early stages there are usually no symptoms, so you can have serious changes while your vision still seems fine.

Screening finds these changes early, when treatment is most effective. Our medical retina service also monitors and treats other retinal conditions without surgery.

At a glance

  • Who needs it Everyone with type 1 or type 2 diabetes
  • How often At least once a year
  • Why Early disease has no symptoms
  • If changes found Monitoring or treatment
02

Symptoms

Early diabetic eye disease is usually silent. Later signs can include:

  • No symptoms at first

    Vision can be normal even with retinal changes.

  • Changing glasses

    Vision that varies with blood sugar levels.

  • Blurred central vision

    From swelling of the macula.

  • Distorted lines

    Straight lines look wavy.

  • Poor night vision

    Difficulty seeing in dim light.

  • Dark spots or floaters

    From bleeding inside the eye.

03

Who Should Be Screened?

Everyone with diabetes should have a retinal check at least once a year, even if their vision is good. You may need checks more often if changes have already been found.

Pregnancy can speed up diabetic eye changes. Women with diabetes should have their eyes checked when planning a pregnancy and during it.

Book a screening if you

  • Have type 1 or type 2 diabetes
  • Have not had a retinal check in the last year
  • Are pregnant or planning a pregnancy and have diabetes
  • Have high blood pressure or kidney disease with diabetes
  • Notice any change in your vision

05

The Screening Visit

  1. 1

    Vision check

    We measure your vision with your glasses.

  2. 2

    Dilating drops

    Drops widen the pupil for a full view of the retina.

  3. 3

    Retinal photos and exam

    Quick photos and an examination of the back of the eye.

  4. 4

    Results and plan

    We explain the findings and when to come back.

06

After Screening

  1. Same day

    Vision is blurry and sensitive to light for 4–6 hours.

  2. Results

    Explained at your visit, with a plan if changes are found.

  3. Next screening

    Usually in 12 months, sooner if needed.

  4. Every day

    Good diabetes control protects your eyes.

Do

  • Bring your glasses and sunglasses
  • Bring your latest sugar results if you have them
  • Arrange transport home
  • Book your next screening

Avoid

  • Driving until the drops wear off
  • Skipping yearly checks when vision is fine
  • Ignoring sudden vision changes
