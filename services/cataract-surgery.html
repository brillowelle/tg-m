What Is a Cataract?

A cataract occurs when the eye’s natural lens becomes cloudy, causing blurred or reduced vision. Cataracts often develop gradually and can affect activities such as reading, driving and recognising faces.

Although cataracts are commonly associated with ageing, they can also develop due to other factors, including eye injuries and certain medical conditions.

02

Symptoms of Cataracts

Common symptoms include:

  • Blurred or cloudy vision

  • Difficulty seeing at night

  • Increased sensitivity to light and glare

  • Halos around lights

  • Colours appearing faded

  • Frequent changes in glasses prescription

Symptoms can vary. A comprehensive eye examination helps identify whether cataracts or another eye condition are affecting your vision.

03

When Is Cataract Surgery Needed?

Cataract surgery may be recommended when cloudy vision begins to interfere with daily activities or when the cataract prevents appropriate examination or treatment of another eye condition.

An ophthalmologist considers your vision, eye health, symptoms and daily needs before recommending surgery.

Not every cataract requires immediate surgery.

05

Cataract Surgery Procedure

Cataract surgery generally involves removing the cloudy natural lens and replacing it with an artificial intraocular lens (IOL).

The procedure typically includes:

  1. 1

    Preoperative Assessment

    Eye examinations and measurements to plan treatment.

  2. 2

    Anaesthesia

    Usually local anaesthesia to keep the eye comfortable.

  3. 3

    Cataract Removal

    The cloudy lens is removed using an appropriate surgical technique.

  4. 4

    Lens Implantation

    An artificial lens is placed inside the eye.

  5. 5

    Postoperative Care

    The patient receives instructions and follow-up arrangements.

The exact procedure depends on the patient’s clinical needs and the surgical techniques available.

06

Recovery After Cataract Surgery

Recovery varies from person to person. Some patients notice improvements in vision within a few days, while complete healing may take several weeks.

Seek urgent medical attention if you experience severe eye pain or sudden loss of vision after surgery.

Your ophthalmologist may recommend:

  • Using prescribed eye drops
  • Avoiding rubbing the operated eye
  • Following instructions about physical activities
  • Attending scheduled follow-up visits
  • Protecting the eye as advised
07

Frequently Asked Questions

Cataract surgery is usually performed with local anaesthesia. Patients may experience pressure or mild discomfort, but significant pain is not expected.

The operation often takes less than an hour, although preparation, observation and the overall clinic visit take longer.

The removed cataract does not grow back. However, clouding of the capsule supporting the artificial lens can develop later and may require treatment.

Recovery times vary. Your ophthalmologist will advise when you can safely resume work, driving and other activities.
