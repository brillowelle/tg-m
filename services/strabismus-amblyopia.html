What Are Squint and Lazy Eye?

Strabismus (squint) is when the eyes do not point in the same direction. One eye may turn in, out, up or down, all the time or only sometimes.

Amblyopia (lazy eye) is reduced vision in one eye because the brain and that eye have not learned to work together properly during childhood. A squint or a big difference in glasses between the eyes can cause it.

Pleoptic treatment strengthens the weaker eye, for example with patching. Orthoptic treatment uses exercises and glasses to help both eyes work together.

At a glance

  • Also called Squint and lazy eye
  • Who it affects Mostly children, some adults
  • Main danger Permanent weak vision in one eye
  • Treatment Glasses, patching, exercises, surgery
02

Signs to Watch For

Signs that may suggest a squint or lazy eye include:

  • An eye that turns

    One eye drifts in, out, up or down.

  • Head tilting or turning

    To use the eyes more comfortably.

  • Double vision

    Seeing two of things, mostly in older children and adults.

  • Closing one eye

    Especially in bright light.

  • Poor reading or clumsiness

    Difficulty judging distance.

  • Weak vision in one eye

    Often found only at an eye test.

03

When is Treatment Needed?

Treatment should start as soon as a squint or lazy eye is found. In children, vision develops until about age 7–8, so early treatment gives the best chance of normal sight.

Children do not grow out of a true squint. If you notice an eye turning, book an eye check rather than waiting.

Book an assessment if

  • You notice an eye turning in your child
  • Your child closes one eye or tilts the head
  • There is a family history of squint or lazy eye
  • An eye test showed weaker vision in one eye
  • An adult develops new double vision

Treatment

  1. 1

    Detailed eye test

    Vision in each eye and how the eyes move together.

  2. 2

    Glasses check

    Drops may be used to measure glasses accurately.

  3. 3

    Treatment plan

    Glasses, patching, exercises, or surgery for some squints.

  4. 4

    Regular reviews

    Progress checks every few weeks to months.

06

During Treatment

  1. First weeks

    Getting used to glasses and the patching routine.

  2. First months

    Vision in the weaker eye usually starts to improve.

  3. Ongoing

    Treatment is adjusted at each review.

  4. Long term

    Some children need follow-up until their teens.

Do

  • Wear glasses all day if prescribed
  • Patch for the hours advised
  • Do exercises regularly
  • Praise and reward your child

Avoid

  • Stopping treatment when it gets hard
  • Waiting for a squint to go away
  • Missing review appointments
07

FAQs

Many squints improve with glasses and exercises. Some still need surgery to straighten the eyes.

It varies from a few months to longer, depending on the age of the child and how weak the eye is.

Adults can be helped with glasses, prisms or surgery for squint and double vision, though lazy eye responds best in childhood.

If treated early it can often be improved a lot. Untreated, the weaker vision may become permanent.
