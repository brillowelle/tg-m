About This Clinic

Our contact lens clinic fits lenses that are comfortable, safe and give clear vision. We also look after people with keratoconus, a condition where the cornea becomes thin and cone-shaped.

In keratoconus, the cornea (the clear front window of the eye) slowly bulges outwards. This bends light unevenly and makes vision blurred and distorted. It often starts in the teenage years and can progress into the thirties.

Glasses often cannot correct keratoconus well. Specially designed contact lenses can give much clearer vision, and regular monitoring helps detect progression early.

At a glance

  • Lenses offered Soft, toric and specialty lenses
  • Keratoconus starts Usually in teens and twenties
  • Main concern Thinning, cone-shaped cornea
  • Care Fitting, monitoring, referral
02

Signs of Keratoconus

Keratoconus can be easy to miss early on. Common signs include:

  • Frequent glasses changes

    Your prescription keeps changing, especially astigmatism.

  • Distorted vision

    Straight lines look bent or wavy.

  • Glare and light sensitivity

    Halos and streaks around lights at night.

  • Ghost images

    Seeing double outlines with one eye.

  • Eye rubbing

    Itchy eyes and frequent rubbing are linked to keratoconus.

  • Blurred vision with glasses

    Even new glasses do not give sharp vision.

03

When to Book

Book an assessment if you want to start wearing contact lenses, are unhappy with your current lenses, or have signs of keratoconus. Young people with rapidly changing astigmatism should be checked.

Rubbing your eyes can make keratoconus worse. If your eyes itch, ask us about treating the allergy instead of rubbing.

Book a lens or cornea check if you

  • Want to switch from glasses to contact lenses
  • Find your current lenses uncomfortable
  • Have high or changing astigmatism
  • Have a family history of keratoconus
  • Have been told your cornea is irregular

06

Wearing Your Lenses

  1. First week

    Build up wearing time slowly as your eyes get used to the lenses.

  2. First review

    We check the fit, your vision and the eye surface.

  3. Every 6–12 months

    Regular lens and eye health checks.

  4. With keratoconus

    Corneal scans to watch for progression.

Do

  • Wash and dry hands before handling lenses
  • Use fresh solution every time
  • Replace lenses on schedule
  • Keep glasses as a backup

Avoid

  • Sleeping in lenses unless advised
  • Using tap water on lenses
  • Swimming or showering in lenses
  • Rubbing your eyes
