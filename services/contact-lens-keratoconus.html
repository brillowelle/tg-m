What Is Keratoconus?

Keratoconus is an eye condition in which the cornea, the clear front surface of the eye, gradually becomes thinner and bulges into a cone-like shape.

This change can affect how light enters the eye, causing blurred or distorted vision. The condition may affect each eye differently and can progress over time.

At a glance

  • Lenses offered Soft, toric and specialty lenses
  • Keratoconus starts Usually in teens and twenties
  • Main concern Thinning, cone-shaped cornea
  • Care Fitting, monitoring, referral
02

Symptoms of Keratoconus

Common symptoms may include:

  • Blurred or distorted vision

  • Increasing difficulty seeing clearly

  • Frequent changes in glasses prescription

  • Increased sensitivity to light or glare

  • Difficulty seeing clearly at night

  • Different vision quality between the two eyes

These symptoms may also occur with other eye conditions. A clinical assessment is needed to identify the cause.

03

When Is Keratoconus Treatment Needed?

Consider arranging an eye examination if you experience persistent blurred vision, distorted vision or frequent changes in your prescription.

An ophthalmologist may recommend further assessment if keratoconus is suspected or if there are signs that the cornea’s shape is changing.

The appropriate management plan depends on the severity of the condition, its progression and your individual visual needs.

Keratoconus Diagnosis and Treatment Options

Keratoconus assessment may involve several examinations to evaluate the cornea and understand how the condition affects vision.

  1. 1

    Vision Assessment

    Checks how clearly you can see and whether your vision has changed.

  2. 2

    Corneal Examination

    Evaluates the cornea for changes in its shape and appearance.

  3. 3

    Corneal Imaging

    Specialised imaging may be used to map the cornea and assess its shape, depending on the equipment available.

  4. 4

    Management Options

    Depending on the condition, management may include glasses, specialised contact lenses or procedures intended to slow progression or address specific visual needs.

Your ophthalmologist will explain the options appropriate for your condition.

06

Living With Keratoconus and Follow-Up Care

Keratoconus may require ongoing monitoring to identify changes in the cornea and vision.

The frequency of follow-up depends on the condition and your clinician’s recommendations.

Your ophthalmologist may recommend:

  • Attending scheduled follow-up examinations
  • Updating vision correction when required
  • Following contact lens care instructions if lenses are prescribed
  • Reporting changes in vision
  • Avoiding eye rubbing and seeking advice about persistent eye irritation
