Why Children’s Eye Care?

Children’s eyes keep developing until about the age of 7–8. Problems found during these years can often be treated fully, but if they are missed they may affect vision for life.

Young children rarely complain about their sight because they think what they see is normal. A child who cannot see well may simply seem uninterested in reading, clumsy or tired.

Our paediatric eye clinic checks vision, eye alignment and eye health in a calm, friendly way, and treats common problems such as short sight, lazy eye (amblyopia) and squint.

At a glance

  • Age range Newborns to teenagers
  • Common problems Short sight, lazy eye, squint
  • Why early Vision develops until age 7–8
  • Treatment Glasses, patching, eye exercises
02

Signs to Watch For

Look out for these signs that your child may have a vision problem:

  • Eyes turning in or out

    One eye drifts, especially when tired.

  • Sitting close

    Holding books near or sitting close to the TV.

  • Squinting or tilting the head

    To see more clearly.

  • Watery or rubbed eyes

    Frequent eye rubbing or tearing.

  • Covering one eye

    Closing or covering one eye to see.

  • Headaches

    Especially after school or reading.

03

When to Book a Check

Every child should have an eye check before starting school, even with no symptoms. Book sooner if you notice any of the signs above or if there is a family history of eye problems.

Lazy eye responds best to treatment before age 7. The earlier it is found, the better the chance of normal vision.

Book a children’s eye check if your child

  • Is starting nursery or school
  • Has an eye that turns or wanders
  • Has a parent or sibling with a squint or lazy eye
  • Was born early or had a low birth weight
  • Is struggling with reading or schoolwork

The Eye Test

  1. 1

    Getting to know your child

    We ask about pregnancy, birth, development and family history.

  2. 2

    Vision test

    Picture or letter charts suited to your child’s age.

  3. 3

    Alignment and eye health

    We check how the eyes move together and look inside the eyes.

  4. 4

    Plan and advice

    Glasses, patching or follow-up, explained to you and your child.

06

After the Visit

  1. Same day

    If drops were used, vision may be blurry and bright for a few hours.

  2. First weeks

    Children usually get used to new glasses within one to two weeks.

  3. Follow-up

    We review progress every few months during treatment.

  4. Every year

    Annual eye checks while your child is growing.

Do

  • Encourage your child to wear glasses all day
  • Follow the patching routine closely
  • Make treatment fun with rewards
  • Keep follow-up visits

Avoid

  • Stopping glasses or patches early
  • Long screen time without breaks
  • Waiting to see if a squint goes away
  • Using adult eye drops for children
