What Is Oculoplastic Care?

Oculoplastic care focuses on conditions affecting the eyelids, the tissues around the eyes and related structures.

These conditions may include eyelid drooping, eyelid lumps, eyelid position problems and other concerns involving the tissues around the eyes. The appropriate treatment depends on the condition and its effect on eye health and function.

At a glance

  • Treats Eyelids, tear ducts, eye socket
  • Common surgeries Blepharoplasty, entropion, lump removal
  • Why it matters Comfort, vision and eye protection
  • Anaesthesia Usually local
02

Common Eyelid and Surrounding Eye Concerns

Conditions that may require assessment include:

  • Drooping upper eyelids

  • Eyelids that turn inward or outward

  • Eyelid lumps or swelling

  • Excess skin around the eyelids

  • Eyelid irritation or discomfort

  • Changes affecting the tissues around the eyes

Not every eyelid concern requires surgery. An assessment helps determine the cause and suitable next steps.

03

When Should You Seek Oculoplastic Assessment?

Consider arranging an eye assessment if you experience persistent eyelid swelling, a lump that does not resolve, changes in eyelid position or difficulty opening your eyes comfortably.

An assessment is particularly important if an eyelid problem interferes with vision or causes ongoing irritation.

Seek urgent medical care for sudden vision changes, severe eye pain, significant eye injury or rapidly developing swelling accompanied by other concerning symptoms.

05

Assessment and Treatment Options

The assessment process depends on the concern being evaluated.

  1. 1

    Eyelid Examination

    The ophthalmologist examines the eyelids and surrounding structures for abnormalities.

  2. 2

    Vision and Eye Health Assessment

    Where relevant, the examination may include checks to determine whether the condition affects vision or eye comfort.

  3. 3

    Treatment Planning

    Management may involve observation, medical treatment or a procedure, depending on the diagnosis.

  4. 4

    Surgical Options

    Surgery may be considered for selected conditions when clinically appropriate. Your ophthalmologist will explain the expected benefits, risks and recovery requirements.

06

Recovery and Aftercare

Recovery varies depending on the condition and whether a procedure is performed.

If treatment or surgery is recommended, your clinical team will explain how to care for the affected area and when to return for follow-up.

Follow the individual instructions provided by your treating clinician.

General aftercare may include:

  • Following prescribed medication instructions
  • Keeping the treated area clean as advised
  • Avoiding rubbing or unnecessary pressure around the eye
  • Attending scheduled follow-up appointments
  • Reporting worsening pain, swelling or changes in vision promptly
