What is Oculoplastic Surgery?

Oculoplastic surgery treats problems of the eyelids, the tear drainage system and the tissues around the eye.

Eyelid problems are not only cosmetic. Droopy, inward-turning or outward-turning lids can block vision, cause irritation and damage the surface of the eye. Blocked tear ducts can cause constant watering and infections.

Our surgeons combine eye health and careful technique, so that the eyelids work properly and look natural.

At a glance

  • Treats Eyelids, tear ducts, eye socket
  • Common surgeries Blepharoplasty, entropion, lump removal
  • Why it matters Comfort, vision and eye protection
  • Anaesthesia Usually local
02

Common Problems

You may benefit from an oculoplastic assessment if you have:

  • Droopy upper eyelids

    Lids that block your upper vision.

  • Eyelids turning in or out

    Lashes rubbing the eye or a lid pulling away.

  • Lumps on the eyelid

    Chalazion, cysts or other growths.

  • Constant watery eyes

    Tears running down the cheek from a blocked duct.

  • Puffy or heavy lids

    Excess skin or bags that make you look tired.

  • Injury around the eye

    Cuts or damage to the eyelids.

03

When is Surgery Needed?

Surgery is recommended when an eyelid or tear duct problem affects your vision, comfort or eye health, or when it does not improve with simpler treatment.

Eyelid problems can harm the eye itself. Lashes rubbing on the cornea can cause scratches and infection, so they should be treated early.

Surgery is usually considered when

  • Drooping lids block your vision
  • Lashes rub against the eye
  • An eyelid lump does not go away
  • Watering continues despite treatment
  • You want to improve tired-looking eyelids

06

Recovery

  1. First days

    Mild swelling and bruising are normal. Use cold compresses.

  2. About 1 week

    Stitches are removed if needed. Bruising fades.

  3. 2–4 weeks

    Most swelling settles and you return to normal activities.

  4. A few months

    Final results appear as scars soften.

Do

  • Use cold compresses as advised
  • Sleep with your head raised
  • Apply ointment as prescribed
  • Keep the wound clean and dry

Avoid

  • Rubbing the eyelids
  • Eye make-up for two weeks
  • Heavy exercise for the first week
  • Swimming until healed
