What is Entropion?

Entropion is a condition in which the eyelid, usually the lower lid, turns inwards so that the lashes rub against the surface of the eye.

It most often happens with age, as the eyelid muscles and tissues become looser. It can also follow scarring, infection or injury.

The constant rubbing can scratch the cornea and lead to ulcers and infection, so entropion should be treated before it damages the eye.

At a glance

  • Affects Usually the lower eyelid
  • Who is at risk Mostly older adults
  • Main danger Scratches and ulcers on the cornea
  • Treatment Minor eyelid surgery
02

Symptoms

Common signs of entropion include:

  • Scratchy, gritty feeling

    Like something is always in the eye.

  • Red, sore eye

    Irritation that does not settle.

  • Watering and discharge

    Tears and mucus from the irritated eye.

  • Sensitivity to light

    Bright light feels uncomfortable.

  • Lashes touching the eye

    Visible inward-turned lashes.

  • Blurred vision

    From damage to the cornea.

03

When is Surgery Needed?

Surgery is recommended once entropion is diagnosed, especially if lashes are touching the cornea. Lubricating drops or tape can relieve symptoms only for a short time.

Do not wait for the cornea to be damaged. Early surgery is simple and prevents scratches that can affect vision.

Surgery is usually advised when

  • Lashes rub on the eye most of the time
  • The eye is constantly red or watery
  • There are signs of corneal scratches
  • Drops and taping no longer help
  • The condition is getting worse

05

Procedure

  1. 1

    Examination

    We check the eyelid, lashes and cornea.

  2. 2

    Local anaesthetic

    The eyelid is numbed with a small injection.

  3. 3

    Lid repositioning

    The eyelid is tightened and turned outward into its normal position.

  4. 4

    Going home

    An eye pad and ointment are applied and you go home the same day.

06

Recovery

  1. First days

    Swelling and bruising are normal. Keep the pad on as advised.

  2. About 1 week

    Stitches are removed if needed. Irritation usually eases quickly.

  3. 2 weeks

    The lid settles into its new position.

  4. Follow-up

    We check the eyelid and the health of the cornea.

Do

  • Use ointment and drops as prescribed
  • Keep the area clean
  • Sleep with your head slightly raised
  • Keep your follow-up visit

Avoid

  • Rubbing the eyelid
  • Heavy lifting for a week
  • Eye make-up for two weeks
  • Swimming until healed

FAQs
