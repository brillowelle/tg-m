About Eye Injuries

Eye injuries can happen at work, at home, on the road or during sport. They range from minor scratches to serious damage inside the eye.

Common injuries include scratches on the cornea, dust or metal particles stuck in the eye, chemical splashes, blows to the eye and cuts to the eyelid.

Even injuries that seem minor can lead to infection or loss of vision if they are not treated properly, so prompt assessment is important.

At a glance

  • Common causes Work, sports, road accidents, home
  • Types Scratches, foreign bodies, blunt injury
  • Main danger Infection and vision loss
  • Treatment Removal, drops, surgical repair
02

Warning Signs

After an eye injury, seek care if you have:

  • Pain or watering

    Ongoing pain or tears after the injury.

  • Blurred or lost vision

    Any drop in vision needs urgent care.

  • Bleeding

    Blood in the white of the eye or behind the cornea.

  • Light sensitivity

    Bright light is painful.

  • Something stuck in the eye

    A feeling of a particle that will not wash out.

  • Eyelid cut

    Cuts on or near the eyelid margin.

03

When to Seek Care

Seek care the same day for any eye injury with pain, blurred vision, bleeding, a cut, or a feeling that something is stuck in the eye.

For chemical splashes, rinse first. Rinse the eye with plenty of clean water for at least 15 minutes, then seek care immediately.

Seek urgent care if

  • Vision is reduced after the injury
  • A chemical has splashed into the eye
  • Something hit the eye at high speed, such as metal
  • The eye is cut or the pupil looks unusual
  • Pain is severe or getting worse

05

Assessment & Treatment

  1. 1

    Vision and history

    We check your vision and ask how the injury happened.

  2. 2

    Examination

    A microscope examination finds scratches, particles and damage.

  3. 3

    Treatment

    Removal of particles, drops, patching or surgical repair.

  4. 4

    Follow-up

    Reviews make sure the eye heals without infection.

06

After Treatment

  1. First days

    Use drops or ointment exactly as prescribed.

  2. Healing

    Most scratches heal within a few days.

  3. Follow-up

    Reviews continue until the eye has fully healed.

  4. Prevention

    Protective eyewear lowers the risk of future injuries.

Do

  • Rinse chemical splashes with water right away
  • Use prescribed drops
  • Wear protective glasses for risky work
  • Keep follow-up visits

Avoid

  • Rubbing an injured eye
  • Removing stuck objects yourself
  • Using old or someone else’s drops
  • Delaying care
07

FAQs

You can gently rinse with clean water, but do not try to pick out stuck particles. Let a doctor remove them safely.

Most scratches heal quickly with treatment, but they can become infected, so they should be checked.

Rinse the eye with plenty of clean water for at least 15 minutes, then seek care immediately.

Wear safety glasses for grinding, drilling, gardening and some sports, and keep chemicals away from children.
