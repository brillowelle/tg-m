What Is a Pterygium?

A pterygium is a growth of tissue on the conjunctiva, the clear membrane covering the white part of the eye. It can gradually extend towards the cornea, the clear front surface of the eye.

Pterygium is often associated with exposure to ultraviolet light, wind, dust and other environmental irritants. The size and effects of the growth can vary between individuals.

At a glance

  • Also called Surfer’s eye
  • Main cause Sun, dust and wind exposure
  • Main danger Can blur vision if it grows
  • Treatment Drops or surgery with autograft
02

Symptoms of Pterygium

Common symptoms may include:

  • Redness on the surface of the eye

  • Irritation or a gritty sensation

  • Dryness or discomfort

  • A visible tissue growth on the white of the eye

  • Blurred vision in some cases

  • Difficulty wearing contact lenses comfortably

Some pterygia cause few symptoms, while others may become more noticeable or affect vision.

03

When Is Pterygium Excision Needed?

Not every pterygium requires surgery. An ophthalmologist may recommend monitoring when the growth causes minimal symptoms and does not affect vision.

Surgical removal may be considered if the pterygium causes persistent irritation, interferes with vision, affects contact lens use or continues to grow towards the cornea.

An examination is necessary to determine whether surgery is appropriate for your condition.

Pterygium Excision Procedure

When surgery is recommended, pterygium excision involves removing the abnormal tissue from the eye’s surface.

The procedure may involve:

  1. 1

    Preoperative Assessment

    The ophthalmologist examines the eye and discusses the procedure.

  2. 2

    Anaesthesia

    Appropriate anaesthesia is used to help keep the patient comfortable.

  3. 3

    Tissue Removal

    The pterygium is surgically removed.

  4. 4

    Surface Management

    The surgeon manages the affected area according to the chosen surgical technique.

  5. 5

    Aftercare

    The patient receives postoperative instructions and follow-up arrangements.

The exact surgical technique and whether additional tissue is used depend on the clinical assessment and the procedure offered by the clinic.

06

Recovery After Pterygium Excision

Recovery varies according to the procedure and the individual. Temporary redness, irritation or discomfort may occur after surgery.

Contact your clinical team if symptoms worsen. Severe pain or sudden changes in vision require urgent medical assessment.

Your ophthalmologist will provide instructions about:

  • Using prescribed eye drops
  • Keeping the eye protected as advised
  • Avoiding rubbing the operated eye
  • Limiting activities as recommended
  • Attending follow-up appointments
