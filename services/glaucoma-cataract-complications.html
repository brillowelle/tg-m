01

What Are These Conditions?

After cataract surgery, the thin capsule that holds the new lens can slowly become cloudy. This is called posterior capsule opacification, or “after-cataract”, and it can make vision blurry again months or years later.

Glaucoma is raised eye pressure that damages the optic nerve. Some types, such as narrow-angle glaucoma, can be treated or prevented with laser.

Both are treated with quick, painless lasers in the clinic: a YAG laser to clear the cloudy capsule, and laser procedures to help fluid drain and lower eye pressure.

At a glance

  • Also called PCO / secondary cataract
  • Who it affects People who had cataract surgery, glaucoma patients
  • Main problem Blurred vision, raised pressure
  • Treatment YAG laser, glaucoma laser
02

Symptoms

You may need assessment if you notice:

  • Vision blurring again

    Months or years after cataract surgery.

  • Glare from lights

    Halos and dazzle, especially at night.

  • Glasses no longer help

    Updating glasses does not sharpen vision.

  • Raised eye pressure

    Found at a routine check.

  • Loss of side vision

    A sign of glaucoma damage.

  • Painful red eye

    Sudden pain needs urgent care.

03

When is Treatment Needed?

YAG laser is recommended when capsule clouding affects your vision. Glaucoma laser is advised when eye pressure is high, or when the drainage angle is narrow and at risk of closing.

After-cataract is not the cataract coming back. It is a common, easily treated clouding of the capsule behind the new lens.

Laser treatment may help if you

  • Had cataract surgery and vision is blurring again
  • Have glare that affects daily life
  • Have raised eye pressure or glaucoma
  • Have narrow drainage angles
  • Want to reduce the number of glaucoma drops

05

Procedure

  1. 1

    Assessment

    We check your vision, eye pressure and the cause of blurring.

  2. 2

    Preparation

    Drops widen or narrow the pupil and numb the eye.

  3. 3

    Laser treatment

    You sit at the laser while short pulses treat the eye.

  4. 4

    Pressure check

    We check your eye pressure before you go home.

06

After Treatment

  1. First hours

    Vision is blurry from the drops. Floaters are common after YAG.

  2. Next day

    Vision usually becomes clearer within a day.

  3. First week

    Use any drops prescribed to control inflammation or pressure.

  4. Follow-up

    A review checks your vision and eye pressure.

Do

  • Use prescribed drops
  • Arrange transport home
  • Report pain or worsening vision
  • Keep follow-up appointments

Avoid

  • Driving until vision clears
  • Rubbing the eye
  • Stopping glaucoma drops unless told to
