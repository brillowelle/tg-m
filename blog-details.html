Understanding Cataract Surgery: Modern Techniques, Recovery & What to Expect in Oman

Dr. Waleed Bunami Consultant Ophthalmologist (20+ Years Exp.)
October 24, 2026 6 min read

Key Takeaways for Patients

  • Cataracts are a normal clouding of your natural crystalline lens, not a growth or film over the eye.
  • Modern surgery (phacoemulsification) uses ultrasonic vibrations to break up the cataract through a tiny 2.2 mm sutureless incision.
  • The procedure takes only 15 to 25 minutes under gentle anaesthetic drops, with no overnight hospital stay.
  • Most patients notice clearer, brighter vision within 24 to 48 hours of the procedure.

Blurry vision, glare when driving at night, and colours that seem yellowish or washed out are common early signs that the natural lens inside your eye is losing its transparency.

At Majan Eye Centre in Barka, cataracts are one of the most frequently diagnosed and successfully treated eye conditions. Thanks to advances in microsurgery and modern intraocular lenses (IOLs), cataract surgery is now among the safest and most life-enhancing procedures performed worldwide.

1. What Causes a Cataract & Why Does It Develop?

Inside each eye sits a clear lens that focuses incoming light onto the retina. As we age, the proteins within this lens begin to clump together, gradually turning a clear window into a frosted pane of glass.

While ageing is the main cause (most people develop some lens clouding after age 60), several other factors can speed up cataract formation:

  • Diabetes: Poorly controlled blood sugar significantly speeds up the breakdown of lens proteins.
  • Strong UV sunlight: Years of bright sun without UV-blocking sunglasses cause cumulative oxidative damage.
  • Eye injury or previous eye surgery: Blunt trauma or past retinal procedures.
  • Long-term steroid medication: Prolonged use of steroid eye drops, tablets, or inhalers.

2. Symptoms You Shouldn't Ignore

Because cataracts usually develop slowly over months or years, many patients adapt without realising how much their vision has changed. Common signs include:

  • Vision that looks cloudy, misty, or like looking through smudged glass.
  • Halos and glare around oncoming headlights when driving at night.
  • Needing brighter light to read the Quran, documents, or medicine labels.
  • Frequent changes in glasses prescription that help less each time.
  • Faded or yellowed colours (whites look cream or brownish).
Clinical Note from Dr. Waleed Bunami

You no longer have to wait for a cataract to become "ripe" before surgery. Today, surgery is recommended once cloudy vision starts to affect your daily safety, driving confidence, or quality of life.

3. How Modern Ultrasound Surgery (Phacoemulsification) Works

The standard procedure at Majan Eye Centre is micro-incision phacoemulsification. Here is what our patients experience, step by step:

  1. Numbing drops: Anaesthetic eye drops are applied. No needles around the eye and no general anaesthesia are needed.
  2. Micro-incision: A tiny self-sealing opening (about 2.2 mm wide) is made at the edge of the clear cornea.
  3. Ultrasound removal: A miniature ultrasonic probe gently breaks up the cloudy lens and removes it.
  4. Foldable lens implant: An artificial intraocular lens (IOL) chosen for your eye is folded, inserted through the same opening, and unfolds inside the natural lens capsule.
  5. No stitches: Because the opening seals itself, stitches are usually not needed.

"The procedure itself usually takes 15 to 20 minutes. Patients stay awake and relaxed, feel no pain, and go home the same day."

— Dr. Waleed Bunami, Consultant Ophthalmologist at Majan Eye Centre

4. Traditional Surgery vs. Modern Micro-Incision Phaco

To appreciate how comfortable today's surgery has become, compare it with the older technique:

Feature Traditional Surgery (Past) Modern Phacoemulsification (Today)
Incision size Large (8 mm – 12 mm) Tiny (1.8 mm – 2.2 mm)
Stitches Usually 5 to 7 stitches No stitches (self-sealing)
Anaesthesia Injection around the eye Numbing eye drops in most cases
Recovery time 6 to 8 weeks 1 to 3 days for functional vision
Astigmatism risk High, due to stitch tension Minimal

5. Recovery Timeline & Post-Operative Care

Caring for your eye after surgery is straightforward. Here is what to expect:

  • Day 1: Wear the clear protective shield while sleeping. Mild scratchiness or watering is normal, and vision is usually noticeably brighter by evening.
  • Days 2 to 7: Use your antibiotic and anti-inflammatory drops exactly as prescribed. Avoid rubbing the eye, swimming, or getting tap water in the eye.
  • Weeks 2 to 4: Return to walking, reading, light exercise, and household chores. Your final glasses check (if reading glasses are needed) is usually around week 4.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a cataract come back after surgery?

No. Once the cloudy lens is replaced with an artificial lens, a cataract cannot grow back. Sometimes the capsule that holds the new lens becomes hazy months or years later (posterior capsular opacification). This is quickly cleared with a painless YAG laser treatment in the clinic.

Are both eyes operated on at the same time?

Usually not. Cataract surgery is normally done one eye at a time, one to two weeks apart, so the first eye can settle and heal before the second.

Will I still need glasses after cataract surgery?

It depends on the lens chosen. Monofocal lenses give clear distance vision (driving, TV), and you may still use reading glasses for small print. Toric lenses can correct astigmatism, while multifocal or extended depth of focus (EDOF) lenses can reduce the need for glasses at both near and far distances.
